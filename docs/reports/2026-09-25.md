- McCarthy, "X-risk Accelerationism" (Theory Decay, Sep 2026) names the paradox
  by which existential-risk discourse reproduces the capacities it identifies
  as threatening while insulating them from democratic contestation: shortened
  time horizons, inevitabilism ("someone will build it, so it should be us"),
  erasure of present harms for hypothetical ones, redistribution of political

  scapegoating latest. This is operationally close to Laclaudian
  equivalence/difference logic and antagonism formation, and offers a testable
  temporal hypothesis for Phase 2 prototypes: is antagonistic signifier
  construction temporally ordered in AI26 data too?

## Research watch

### High signal: the frozen-codebook division of labor gets its strongest evidence yet

arXiv:2609.25447 (21 Sep), "Conduct Under Pressure: What Sixty Language Models
Do When a User Pushes": 60 models from 13 vendors, frozen multi-turn pressure
scenes, codebook built by open coding and then frozen. Two separable findings:
whether a model holds its position tracks generation recency (fold rate
correlates with a public capability index at Spearman −0.64, little vendor
effect), while how it holds tracks vendor (six of 17 manner codes sort by
vendor at corrected permutation p ≤ 0.001). The methods result is the important
one for AI26: six LLM coders from three vendors applied the frozen codebook
more consistently than three human coders (Krippendorff's α 0.66 vs 0.46),
agreed with the codebook author at κ 0.84–0.91 on transcripts the codebook's
examples never touched, and matched an adjudicated human reference at 0.83 —
while blind machine readings recovered the codebook's categories without being
able to tell which a second reader would apply the same way. The authors'
division of labor: humans author and bound the codes and own a small reference
set; machines produce labels at volume.

**Why it matters:** this is the strongest empirical support so far for the
locked Phase 1 posture — researcher-authored frozen codebook, LLM output as
provisional pre-analysis, human adjudication on a bounded reference set. It
also sharpens the validation pilot: consistency of LLM application is not the
same as stable interpretability, and "categories recovered but application
unstable" is exactly the failure mode the error-type battery should detect.

Secondary items (lower signal): arXiv:2607.28890 (covered 20 and 23 September)
remains the reference for agreement-is-not-quality; Co-Refine (arXiv:2604.19309)
adds a ±0.15 deterministic grounding constraint on LLM consistency scores —
incremental, useful only as a pattern for bounding LLM-judged scores with
deterministic metrics; an enthymeme-annotation resource (arXiv:2606.12186)
preserves annotator disagreement instead of collapsing it to majority vote and
finds models trained on disagreement outperform majority-vote-trained ones — a
nice precedent for preserving claim status and disagreement rather than
resolving them prematurely.

### High signal: AI politics — "safety" differentiates into five demand formations

Three developments, all 23–25 September, none previously covered:

- **A prohibitionist legislative flank.** AOC and nine House Democrats
  co-sponsored the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act (Sanders/Casar
  framework): permanent prohibition of superhuman AI development, explicit ban
  on recursive self-improvement, a development pause until a federal regulator
  sets safety protocols, a Cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence,
  penalties up to 20 years and a corporate death penalty. Not yet formally
  introduced (no bill number as of 24 September). It crystallizes a three-way
  map: acceleration (White House "AI Force", UNGA address against international
  constraints) vs managed integration (Great American AI Act, June 2026:
  nationalized oversight plus partial state preemption) vs prohibition (this
  bill), with Sanders framing it as a 2028 primary litmus test. The BC
  government's lawsuit against OpenAI over the Tumbler Ridge school shooting
  (product-safety and criminal-coaching framing) feeds the same pressure.

  counterweighted by $100M+ raised by an industry PAC against regulation
  (~$24M spent in H1). Notably cross-partisan: $7M announced for
  pro-regulation Republicans, donors describing single-issue AI-safety voting,
  and Democratic strategists reportedly advising battleground candidates to
  avoid the AI-regulation topic to escape PAC retaliation. At least six likely
  2028 candidates called for regulation this month ("strict guardrails",
  "dangerous on the level of nuclear weapons", a "kill switch" inside the

- **UN Security Council briefing (23 September), now with content.** The
  OpenAI, Anthropic and Hugging Face CEOs plus the UN scientific panel's

  security agenda. Anthropic proposed an international incident-notification

  "transparency"): prohibitionist, donor-driven regulation, lab arms-control
  proposals, industry anti-regulation, and climate/commons antagonism.

**Why it matters for AI26:** this is unusually rich sampling material. The same
signifiers are being articulated into distinct demand formations with different

built to code. Evidence level: the bills, donations, UNSC briefing, summit
outcome and protests are reported facts; the five-formation reading is
interpretation; the durability and internal coherence of the prohibitionist
flank and donor bloc are open questions.
