### Scientific research: two adds worth registering (both predate the morning's window — the
arXiv cs.CY feed has published nothing since 10-09, and the 16:05 section listed the papers that
were visible then; these two were not among them and remain relevant to Phases 1–2)

- **`2610.12701` (8-9 Oct) *Activating the Annotator: Operationalising Lived Experience in Data
  Annotation*** (Munir, Kommiya Mothilal & Ahmed; verified via arXiv API this run) — **credible
  (preprint)**. Introduces AXLE, a pre-annotation stage in which a scenario vignette anchors a
  short, axis-guided conversation synthesised into a first-person micro-narrative returned to the
  annotator before labelling. In a 2x2 study (104 annotators, 30 items, 1,040 annotations) on
  ambiguous hate speech it raised identity resonance and harm sensitivity while **increasing
  label entropy and lowering inter-annotator agreement** — the authors read it as surfacing
  plurality rather than manufacturing consensus. Why it matters: AI26's human-in-the-loop
  validation treats disagreement as information, not noise; AXLE is a concrete, citable design
  for the codebook/annotation protocol that operationalises that stance — and a measured
  trade-off (plurality up, agreement down) that §4.2 should cite when justifying why agreement
  is not the target metric on subjective tasks. Phase 1 validation design; Phase 2 method.
- **`2610.12878` (9 Oct) *Beyond the Monolithic Interface: A Research Agenda for Plural
  Conversational AI*** (Ghosh, Kashif, Kohout & Qi; verified via arXiv API this run) — the
  chatbot's single-voice default produces cognitive offloading, **ideological narrowing** and
  **epistemic compression** of value diversity; multi-persona interfaces are proposed as the
  countermethod. Why it matters: the three harms are Laclaudian by construction — an interface
  that articulates one voice narrows the space of articulable positions; plural personas are
  roughly an interface-level scheme of difference. Useful theoretical vocabulary for Phase 2's
  platform/algorithm layer, and a background cite when interpreting corpus concentration (a
  corpus dominated by single-voice feeds measures exactly the narrowing it predicts).
