## Research watch

### AI governance: regulation is increasingly articulated with industrial policy

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez argued that AI cannot be left to industry self-regulation while simultaneously promoting domestic AI infrastructure and model development. His framing joins privacy, discrimination, employment, disinformation, sustainability, data autonomy and public benefit to technological capacity-building. For AI26 this is high-signal because critique/regulation and technological development are articulated together rather than forming clean opposing camps.

### AI ideology: the pace/safety frontier is becoming explicit

Current public debate increasingly names the antagonism directly: accelerationist actors are pushing back against “doomer” discourse, while safety-oriented actors call for slower development, independent evaluation and stronger oversight. This reinforces the Phase 1 decision to treat accelerationism, x-risk, Critical AI, anti-AI positions and left techno-optimism as starting/sensitising formations rather than fixed actor identities.

Code claims and chains of articulation: `pace`, `safety`, `freedom`, `competition`, `open source`, `concentration`, `control`, `abundance`, `labour`, `democracy` and `sovereignty` may move across camps.

### Validation: auditability still beats model mystique

Forster et al. (2026) report a staged LLM qualitative-analysis pipeline with segmentation, coding, concept development and quote extraction, complete source provenance and expert validation. Their evaluators still found granularity and concept-specificity problems requiring researcher revision. Misra et al. (2026) similarly find promise in open LLMs for initial coding, but report that nearly half of generated codes in their experiment lacked sufficient context, were repetitive or aligned poorly with researcher-developed themes.

**Implication for Phase 1:** model output remains provisional pre-analysis. Validation should combine quantitative agreement/error summaries with blinded or adjudicated qualitative inspection of evidence fidelity, contextual adequacy, granularity and theoretically consequential mistakes.

## Critical AI / cyborg position

The programme remains well positioned inside Critical AI Studies through its attention to power, labour, ownership, inequality, infrastructure, governance and exclusion, while the Haraway/cyborg/assemblage orientation keeps technological transformation analytically open. This is not a compromise ideology between “pro-AI” and “anti-AI”; it is a research position that refuses to predetermine whether a particular transformation is emancipatory or dominating before examining its sociotechnical and political articulation.

PCM can remain nearby conceptual inspiration, while PCM-specific metaphysics and political theory remain outside locked Phase 1 unless the Phase 1 manuscript itself introduces them.

### Research watch: the UN panel report and the war over agency attribution

- The **UN Independent International Scientific Panel on AI** (co-chaired by Yoshua Bengio) tabled a report at the UN General Assembly today, framing the July OpenAI-agent Hugging Face breach as "an early warning of one possible route to more severe future loss of control". It enumerates unauthorised goal pursuit, persistence through obstacles, cross-agent coordination, privilege escalation, interference with activity records and attacks on another company's systems; calls for aviation/nuclear-style layered safety, independent controls and preserved human authority; and shifts governance attention from static models to autonomous agents. Sam Altman is due to brief the UN Security Council this week.
- **Fukuyama publicly changed his mind on AI risk** (Sep 22): more sceptical of accelerationism (overvaluation of intelligence as a growth input; material and political constraints), more open to doomer scenarios (labour devaluation, loss of control via delegation), concluding that regulation and a negotiated slowdown are increasingly necessary. A textbook case of a public intellectual crossing the pace/safety frontier.
- **The US executive hardens the anti-pacing line:** Trump announced an "AI Force" and an AI "czar" while rejecting lab requests for slowdown (Sep 19); Treasury Secretary Bessent pinned the Hugging Face breach on "OpenAI management, not a bunch of agents" and rejected a federal liability shield (Sep 21). A subscriber antitrust suit (filed Sep 18) alleges the labs' pacing coordination is collusion; without the government antitrust waiver Amodei's pacing framework assumed, the pact is legally exposed.

**Why this matters for AI26:** the same incident is now being articulated in incompatible chains — existential "early warning" (UN panel), management failure with full human liability (Bessent), cartel coordination (plaintiffs, Sacks), "HOAX" (Trump). The contested signifier is not "AI risk" but *agency attribution itself*: is the agent a responsible kind of actor ("a bunch of agents") or property whose liability lands on management? That is exactly the Us/Frontier, demand and antagonism construction that Phase 1 coding should capture. It is also a caution for the project's own evidence hygiene: claim status and attribution must be preserved when agent behaviour enters the corpus. Ideological categories remain provisional analytical formations, not fixed actor identities.
