### New finding 1: the `ai_governance` scholarly query is filling the parliamentary arena with machine-learning papers

The parliamentary arena holds **309 records, of which 124 (40.1%) come from the single
`ai_governance` scholarly query**. Reading all 124 titles: **only about 5 (4%) are
governance or policy material** in any sense the paper's §4.1 would recognise, and none
concern parliaments, parties, elections or legislation. The rest are technical papers -
`FurE: Efficient Instance-Specific 3D Fur Reconstruction`, `Stacked Fluid Metasurfaces:
Mutual-Coupling-Aware Modeling and Optimization`, `Quantum Advantage for Distributed
Symmetry Breaking`, `DexTacWAM: A Visuo-Tactile World-Action Model for Dexterous
Manipulation`, `OC-GS: Gaussian Splatting for Irregular Turntable Capture`.

The cause is in the tracked plan (`configs/studies/ai26.sources.example.toml`, row
`ai_governance`): `query = "artificial intelligence AND (governance OR regulation OR audit
OR evaluation OR liability OR standards)"`, `arena = "parliamentary"`. Because "evaluation"
and "audit" appear in the abstract of essentially every ML paper, the query is a firehose
rather than a governance filter.

Consequence for the paper: strip the contaminated layer and **parliamentary falls from
17.4% to 10.4% of the corpus** (185 records). The three-arena comparison in RQ2 is
therefore partly an artefact of one over-broad query, and the parliamentary arena's
scholarly layer - the only part of it that is not government press release - effectively
does not exist. Opened as **#295**.
