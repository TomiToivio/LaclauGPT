## AI26 coverage gaps and missingness accounting (2026-11-21)

**The number the paper needs is the new-document rate, not the sync rate.** Over the last two
complete days before the break (14 and 15 November, 24 cycles each) the stage synced 952 and 944
records, but only 65 and 41 of those were new - the rest are re-syncs and updates of records already
in the store. So the outage costs roughly 950 sync events per day but only about 50 new documents per
day, and the 114 h gap is on the order of **4,500 sync events and ~250 genuinely new documents**. The
sync count overstates the loss; the new-document count understates it wherever the record is the only
capture of a moment (see next paragraph).

**What is inside the gap window.** 18-19 November is the densest elite/grassroots event cluster of
the quarter: the Anthropic IPO's first day of trading, the coordinated "MisAnthropic" statement
(below), follow-through on the SAFE AI Act outline, and protest at the listing. Section 4.1 obliges
the study to document missing material. None of this week is in the corpus, and the 17-21 November
window now needs an entry in the sampling record as a declared observation gap, not only a ticket.

**Recovery feasibility by source family** (read from this repo's collectors, not measured against a
live store):
- bluesky account rows use `app.bsky.feed.getAuthorFeed` with cursor paging - a gap-window refetch is
  technically possible without new API access;
- mastodon account rows use `limit` with a `max_id` cursor - same conclusion;
- scholarly queries are date-filtered, so re-running with the gap window is possible;
- RSS carries only what the feed still holds: days for daily feeds, hours for high-volume ones;
- `web_source` index pages are largely ephemeral.

Consequence: the two families that produced 67% of everything synced in the ten days to 16 November
(bluesky 1,139 and mastodon 620 of 2,630 non-browser records) are the two that can be re-fetched. That
is exactly the evidence #440 needs before the window is declared permanently lost.

**Concentration, so the loss is read as the loss of a narrow stream.** 29 distinct source rows
produced any outcome in those ten days. Two Mastodon accounts and two scholarly queries account for
~44% of everything synced (312 + 308 + 268 + 260 of 2,630). The stream that stopped is narrow, mostly
English and heavy on critical-AI voices; the outage freezes that narrow tail as the corpus's recent
edge, and the five-record caps of #441 are the mechanism.
