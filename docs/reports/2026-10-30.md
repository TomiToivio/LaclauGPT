### Coverage against what Phase 1 actually needs

The paper asserts no corpus counts (`paper/PHASE_1_PAPER.md` has no numbers to contradict), so
nothing here falsifies a published claim. The constraints bite when results are written:

- **Arena balance is 3.2:1 elites to grassroots.** §4.1 promises a three-arena comparison; two
  of the three are a fifth of the corpus. Any "the imaginary travelled from elite to grassroots"
  claim would rest on strata of very unequal depth.
- **Finnish depth, by arena**: elites 17, grassroots 69, parliamentary 51 `fi` records. §4.1
  weights Finland, §4.2 requires an evaluation sample stratified by arena and language. A
  Finnish stratum of that sample can be illustrative only; it cannot support a Finnish arena
  comparison. This refines yesterday's 3.7%-of-ready figure with the arena breakdown.
- **The video layer is empty by design**: youtube, tiktok, instagram are 0 records, deferred by
  `stage_reachability` to the browser node. That is the layer where grassroots labour and
  creator mobilisation mostly live, so the arena that is already thinnest is also the one whose
  main platform is absent. This is the largest structural gap in the design, and it is a
  deployment decision, not a config row.
- **Language labels**: 2,281 records (23.2%) carry none. On the ready set it is 5.7%, so
  stratification is not blocked - but any share computed over the full population silently
  drops a quarter of it.

## AI26 external signals (2026-10-30)

New since the 2026-10-29 register. The House Democrats' 48-hour letters, the "is this even
misalignment" dispute, Anthropic's S-1 unpacking and arXiv 2610.35179 are **not** restated.

- **arXiv 2609.22133v2, "Observational Equivalence of LLM and Human Annotation"** (v2 dated
  28 Oct 2026). Replicating binary classification tasks from 14 peer-reviewed political science
  studies with 13 LLMs, three trained coders and 165 crowd coders under identical codebooks:
  recent LLMs agree with attentive humans at rates comparable to human-human agreement, and
  cross-LLM disagreement concentrates on the texts that also divide humans. The authors argue
  ambiguity in texts and coding rules is the shared source of disagreement, and propose
  cross-LLM disagreement as a difficulty signal and ambiguity-aware bounds for downstream
  inference. *Established as a preprint.* This is the most consequential item of the day for
  §4.2, because it argues against the premise that human labels take precedence whenever humans
  and LLMs differ - the paper's framing should either answer it or adopt its bounds.
- **arXiv 2610.27937, "From DQI to C-DQI: Automatic Codebook Generation and Evaluation"**.
  Top-down versus inductive codebook generation for parliamentary discourse quality, 100
  Knesset transcripts; inductive schemas reach higher inter-model agreement while keeping
  validity. *Preprint; relevant to the parliamentary arena and to the codebook work behind #53.*
- **New York RAISE Act 2.0 (S.10731), introduced 29 Oct 2026** (Gounardes/Wright). State
  authority to "pace the frontier" by capping compute devoted to training or automated AI
  research, quarterly reporting on recursive self-improvement, 72-hour reporting of risk jumps,
  a state-licensed "frontier examiner" with 90-day risk assessments, and penalties up to
