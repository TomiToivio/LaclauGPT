## 3. Scientific research — new high-signal items

- **Validation-first LLM annotation — the strongest methods result seen in this
  watch.** McLaren, Cross, Krakowska, Rauner & Schoonvelde, *"Magic Words or
  Methodical Work? Challenging Conventional Wisdom in LLM-Based Political Text
  Annotation"* (arXiv:2603.26898, v2 2026-03-31): controlled evaluation of six
  open-weight models across four political-science annotation tasks under
  identical quantisation, hardware and prompt-template conditions. Central
  finding: **interaction effects dominate main effects** — pipeline choices are
  consequential researcher degrees of freedom; no single model, prompt style or
  learning approach is uniformly superior; model size is an unreliable guide to
  cost *and* performance; widely recommended prompt-engineering techniques have
  inconsistent, sometimes negative effects. The paper then proposes a
  validation-first framework: principled ordering of pipeline decisions, prompt
  freezing, held-out evaluation, reporting standards.
  **Relevance:** this is a direct external warrant for LaclauGPT's
  validation-protocol and prompt-provenance commitments, and an operational
  guide for Phase 2's model/prompt matrix. [Phase 1 validation, Phase 2]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26898
- **Political propaganda on an agent-only platform.** *"Large-Scale Analysis of
  Political Propaganda on Moltbook"* (arXiv:2603.18349, v2): 673,127 posts and
  879,606 comments by 93,714 AI agents; LLM classifiers validated against expert
  annotation (Cohen's κ ≈ 0.64–0.74). Findings: propaganda is 1% of posts but
  42% of all *political* content; production is highly concentrated (70% of
  posts in five communities; 4% of agents produce 51% of propaganda posts,
  with semantic re-posting within and across communities) while agent responses
  are predominantly neutral. **Relevance:** the first large-scale picture of
  purely agent-populated political communication — exactly the "artificial
  communication" layer of Phase 2, and a natural Phase 3 comparison point for
  production-vs-amplification asymmetries. [Phase 2, Phase 3]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18349
- **Synthetic narrative coordination as measurable multi-signal construct.**
  *"Detecting Synthetic Political Narratives in Cross-Platform Social Media
  Discourse"* (arXiv:2605.21540): four coordination signals (lexical diversity,
  temporal burstiness, rhetorical repetition, semantic homogenization) composed
  into one score, applied to 353,223 records across six event windows
  (2023–2026); crucial negative result: **no single indicator is sufficient —
  the language of the corpus is itself a confound** (the Russian-language
  channel ranks last on every window despite high homogenization because
  lexical diversity and cross-channel Jaccard are language-dependent).
  **Relevance:** Phase 2 method note on interpreting coordination signals in
  multilingual AI26 arenas; the "criticism ≠ coordination ≠ antagonism"
  discipline applies equally here. [Phase 2]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21540
- **Nearby implementation reference, not a dependency.** *"Agentic Analysis for
  Agentic Infrastructure"* (arXiv:2606.26203): LLM annotation + topic discovery
  + three network analyses (SNA, DNA, socio-semantic bipartite) over 4,323
  governance participation records comparing ERC-8004 and Google A2A protocols.
  A working demonstration of a pipeline structurally similar to the Phase 2
  graph stack (LLM coding → stance → DNA/SNA networks). Useful as a comparison
  point when publishing Phase 2's own contract; not a dependency.
  [Phase 2]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26203
- Carried from the morning edition (still valid, not restated in full): the
