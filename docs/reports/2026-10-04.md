### Scientific research watch — newly surfaced, high signal

Two preprints no prior edition covered; both are validation-side and land
inside the locked Phase 1 framework without extending it:

- **LLM and human coding are observationally equivalent in annotation
  quality (arXiv:2609.22133, Nakamura, Tan and Yean).** Replicates
  text-classification tasks from 14 peer-reviewed political-science
  studies with ten LLMs, three human experts and 165 crowdworkers under
  identical codebooks, and finds LLM-expert agreement comparable to
  expert-expert agreement, with both driven by the same textual and
  coding-rule ambiguity; clarifying the rules reduces disagreement
  among both. It argues the central question is no longer
  human-vs-machine but writing coding rules that minimise ambiguity,
  and proposes using cross-LLM disagreement to find hard cases, with
  ambiguity-aware bounds for downstream inference. This is the
  strongest published support yet for two Phase 1 commitments at once:
  (a) the codebook-first, evidence-linked, human-review pipeline
  (reliability is achieved by procedure, not by model trust), and (b)
  treating LLM disagreement as signal — the position the 2026-10-03
  brief already drew from Kim and Mitchell (arXiv:2609.11109). Its
  ambiguity-aware downstream-bounds idea is directly reusable in
  Phase 2's cross-layer validity battery. Cite in the validation
  section when the paper is next touched; no framework change needed.
  Credible preprint (political methodology; 14 published replications;
  expert and crowd reference sets).
- **LLM Judge Validation Under Sparse Overlap (arXiv:2609.31857,
  NeurIPS 2026).** Proves that in LLM-as-judge validation, overlap
  sparsity between annotation rounds is the first-order determinant of
  wrong deployment decisions (at 5 percent pairwise overlap: 25 percent
  wrong-decision rate; 65 percent chance of selecting the wrong best
  judge among ten candidates); derives a minimum-overlap formula
  (rho >= 0.25 suffices only for non-borderline judges) and shows a
  zero-cost stratified allocation halves false-rejection rates versus
  random sampling. Directly actionable for AI26's human-reference
  sampling design: if human review is budgeted by random sampling of
  items, the budget can be spent badly; stratify by predicted-code
  strata and by coder overlap. Both the Phase 1 validation protocol and
  Phase 2's cross-layer validity battery need an overlap-design note.
  Peer-reviewed venue; the highest-credibility methodological signal
  today.

Context already in the file: the agreement-is-not-validity line
(arXiv:2609.11144; AMALIA, arXiv:2607.08731) is now joined from the
opposite side by arXiv:2609.22133 — human coders are themselves
unreliable under ambiguous rules, so single-human gold labels are a
weak validation target. The Phase 1 validation battery's design
(agreement plus agreement-with-reference plus characteristic-error
analysis, never agreement alone) survives this convergence unmodified;
no action beyond a citation update.
