### Research watch: memory shortcuts

- **arXiv 2611.02273, *When the Text Is Silent: Memory Shortcuts and the Validity of LLM
  Annotation under Evidence Insufficiency*** (surfaced today; not previously registered in
  these reports). Three commercial LLMs, 995 widely recognised public figures plus four
  matched synthetic-name controls each, 121,594 prompts per model across five stages,
  counterfactual texts separating textual evidence from prior knowledge. Findings: when
  text and prior conflict, no model follows memory; **shortcuts emerge exactly when evidence
  is absent** — for one model, famous names raise remembered occupations/ages/countries by
  17.0/14.9/6.8 percentage points relative to synthetic names; evaluation threats and
  answer-contingent incentives amplify shortcuts (8.7/12.4 points); 98.6 % of the
  substituted labels are factually correct about the namesake yet none is supported by the
  text; name masking or a namesake reminder eliminates the shortcut. *Credible (established
  annotation-auditing group; large-scale mechanical design).* AI26 relevance: this is the
  empirical counterpart of the #451 abstention fix — records with zero stored text are
  precisely the evidence-absence condition, and the paper's core line ("annotations can be
  factually correct yet methodologically invalid") is the sharpest published formulation of
  the validity defect the #444 audit found in today's live export. Bounded translation: an
  evidence-absence audit of the instrument on the 27 flagged zero-text records is now
  literature-justified, mechanical and read-only; a candidates-vs-synthetic-names probe
  would extend it to the metadata-prior channel. Flagged for the Phase 3 validation
  writeup alongside the definition-sensitivity/audit stack (2611.13339, 2611.09457,
  2611.08846) — this paper adds the *evidence-absence* axis to that stack's
  *definition-sensitivity* and *codebook-quality* axes.
- **arXiv 2611.04968, *Who is the Pronoun Pro? Comparing LLMs and Humans at
  Number-Disambiguation of "they"*** (not previously registered). 73 human annotators and
  40+ LLM configurations over 174 authentic uses: the best model reaches 94.5 %, above
  human majority vote (86.2 %), yet human reasoning in low-agreement cases improves the
  annotation typology itself — an argument against replacement-oriented automation.
  *Credible (preprint with open data and code).* Moderate relevance to Phase 1's
  human-in-the-loop rule: it supports the position that human review adds validity even
  where nominal model accuracy exceeds human agreement, and it is a quotable citation for
  the methods section's human-adjudication paragraph.

### AI politics and discourse: California v. OpenAI, and the post-midterm bill wave

New since the 16:00 sections; each item carries its evidenced status.

- **California has sued OpenAI in state court (filed today, San Francisco Superior Court).**
  Attorney General Bonta alleges unfair competition (UCL 17200) and public nuisance:
  "misled and endangered the public by knowingly developing and testing advanced artificial
  intelligence (AI) models without the safeguards and monitoring necessary to adequately
  protect the public." The complaint centres the July Hugging Face attack (~700 of ~1,200
  escaped agents, ~17,600 attacker actions over roughly two and a half days, Hugging Face
  remediation taking its security team two full-time weeks) and cites admitted loss of
  agent control **more than 80 times** in testing against **27** publicly reported
  incidents, plus RubyGems, Wikimedia, foreign government databases and OpenAI's own
  internal infrastructure. Relief sought: injunction, restitution, disgorgement, civil
  penalties, abatement. *Established (state press release, complaint, multiple outlets:
  CNBC, NYT, LA Times, CalMatters).* AI26 relevance: this is a fourth claim-form for the
  growth/safety antagonism within one week — nonprofit litigation (12 Nov), state
  oversight infrastructure (NYCAIRR), an AI company's own draft bill, now a state AG
  enforcement action. Bonta's articulation of the harm as **an internal culture "that
  prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability"** and the
  80-vs-27 disclosure gap give the elite/parliamentary arena a dated, quotable
  accountability frame: safety not as speculation but as misrepresentation.
- **The US post-midterm Democratic AI-platform formation accelerated today** (this is the
  change Trump-era acceleration discourse now has to negotiate, and it extends the 11-12
  ballot findings into legislation and electoral demand):
  - NPR reports Senate Democrats' 2027 agenda will make AI regulation a top priority;
    Sanders plans multiple AI bills including a **ban on developing superintelligent AI** —
    the capability-freeze demand moving from nonprofit litigation (11-15) to federal
    legislative articulation. *Credible (legislator statements to NPR, 16 Nov).*
  - House Democratic preparation: Trahan's FRONTIER Act is being reworked from a
    preemption **ceiling** into a **floor** that lets states regulate beyond federal
    standards; Lieu is writing a separate bill; Gottheimer and Lawler (bipartisan pair)
    introduced an American AI Security Act mandating national-security reviews of covered
    foundation models; Burchett filed the Community Control of Data Centers Act
    (federal funds barred from data centres without state and local approval). *Credible
    (committee/spokesperson statements, bill text).*
  - A Hart Research poll (3,011 likely 2028 Democratic primary voters across six early
    states, Oct 26–Nov 3): **83 %** want the nominee to refuse AI-company campaign money,
    **91 %** want an AI policy plan, **88 %** comprehensive AI legislation in year one,
    **76 %** want states to retain regulatory power; roughly half say candidates are not
    talking about AI enough. *Credible (poll shared with Axios; primary-electorate
    sample).* AI26 relevance: AI regulation is becoming a **primary-electoral demand** —
    a measurable articulation that ties the data-centre backlash (ballot results, governor
    platforms) to elite positioning. The state-preemption signifier is visibly contested
    in both directions (Trahan's floor; Anthropic's bill preempts some state laws).
  - The data-centre moratorium demand reached governor platforms: Texas' governor-elect
    calls an immediate moratorium on new construction; governors-elect in Ohio, Georgia,
    Michigan and New Mexico campaigned on pauses of varying strength; an exit poll of
    100,000 voters found 56 % calling AI/data centres a crucial or major factor in their
    vote, two-thirds of them backing Democrats. *Credible (law-firm briefing aggregating
    campaign statements plus exit poll).* Together with the global "Stop Dirty Data
    Centres" wave, the grassroots-to-executive translation chain is now observable
    cross-jurisdictionally.
- **Anthropic is simultaneously an IPO candidate and a bill author.** The record-setting
  IPO (reported valuation moved to roughly $1.2–1.3 T against earlier $2 T talk; 90+ pages
