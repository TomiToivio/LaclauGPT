## Research watch

### AI governance: competition and shared safety are becoming simultaneous articulations

Today's strongest empirical signal is the U.S.–China proposal for an AI-incident notification mechanism. Reporting describes safety and national-security dialogue occurring alongside intense competition for AI dominance. This is excellent AI26 material because `safety`, `control`, `national security`, `competition`, `transparency` and `cooperation` are being articulated simultaneously rather than belonging neatly to separate ideological camps.

For Phase 1, this reinforces claim-level and relational coding. Do not infer that safety language makes an actor “doomer”, or that competition language makes an actor straightforwardly accelerationist. Ask what political project the relation among these signifiers constructs.

The current industry debate has the same structure: Anthropic advocates pacing and external evaluation while Nvidia argues for faster development, yet both positions coexist inside a shared assumption that AI is socially transformative. This is a useful case for studying political frontiers around pace, authority and acceptable risk without reducing the field to stable tribes.

### LLM-assisted qualitative analysis: provenance and hybrid judgement remain the convergence point

Forster et al.'s 2026 multi-stage qualitative pipeline remains directly relevant: segmentation, coding, concept development and quote extraction are separated into auditable stages with expert validation. A newer August 2026 JAMIA study compares human-led and LLM-assisted hybrid deductive–inductive workflows across 122 transcripts and manually verifies quotation fidelity. Together these strengthen Phase 1's existing direction rather than demanding methodological change: keep stages inspectable, preserve quotations/source links, and evaluate model assistance through human judgement rather than agreement scores alone.

### Critical AI / cyborg position

The overall programme remains well positioned inside Critical AI Studies while preserving a Haraway-style openness toward human–technical transformation. Power, labour, ownership, inequality, infrastructure, governance and exclusion remain objects of critique, but technological transformation is not predetermined to be either emancipation or domination. This productive tension belongs at programme/Phase 2 level. PCM can remain nearby conceptual inspiration, while PCM-specific metaphysics and political theory stay outside locked Phase 1 unless the Phase 1 paper itself introduces them.
