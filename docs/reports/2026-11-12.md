- **arXiv 2611.13339, *Measuring Definition Sensitivity in LLM Annotation of Social Science Concepts***
  (11 Nov, cs.CL, NeurIPS 2026 TAE workshop). The model annotates the same texts twice with two competing
  definitions of one concept; the disagreement is "definition sensitivity", and paraphrasing one definition
  unchanged gives a "paraphrase floor". Across six concepts and three LLMs, **swapping definitions always moves
  more annotations than paraphrasing**, and the authors argue any annotation set must be reported with the
  definition that produced it, plus the paraphrase floor. *Established (abstract read).*
  This is the most directly useful methods item in weeks for §4.2: the populist formula has several
  theoretically defensible operationalisations (Laclau's own, Moffitt's, Mudde's, Palonen-style scoring), so
  AI26's coding is definition-sensitive by construction. The paper can report the definition used and a
  paraphrase floor at low cost, and the codebook already carries the definition text.
- **arXiv 2611.13525, *EJAI: Judgment-Preserving Civic AI for Data Center Environmental Justice***
  (11 Nov, cross-list cs.HC/cs.CY). A source-grounded civic tool over the evidence residents actually face in
  data-centre disputes - metrics, corporate claims, policy documents, public testimony - with missing-data
  statements and two stakeholder personas. Their formative finding is a warning for us as much as a
  comparator: dashboards and single-agent AI "settle questions before people examine that evidence", and the
  corporate persona omitted conflicting evidence. *Credible (preprint, abstract read; 21-query benchmark,
  15 participants).* Relevant to the grassroots data-centre stratum and to our own `:8501` presentation.

- **arXiv 2611.09457, *Definitions, Label Names, or Demonstrations? Auditing What Governs
  LLM Annotation*** (Tai, 6 Nov, cs.CL, preprint). A counterfactual audit that reassigns

- **arXiv 2611.08846, *When F1 Is Not Enough: Auditing Codebooks for LLM-Assisted
