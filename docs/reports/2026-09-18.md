## Research watch

### Methods: reproducibility is not construct validity (new; directly relevant to Phase 1 validation)

New arXiv work (2609.19866, 17 Sep) tests LLM annotations of the European Commission's AI Act consultation against survey measures from the same stakeholders: annotation reproducibility is extremely high (intraclass correlations > 0.99), yet convergence with survey-reported measures of the nominal construct is limited, and divergence varies systematically across stakeholder groups. This is the strongest recent empirical support for the Phase 1 validation design: agreement metrics alone are insufficient; human reference standards and construct-level checks are required.

Two further papers refine the same agenda. Kim & Mitchell (2609.11109, 10 Sep) quantify multi-agent LLM qualitative coding: accuracy depends on codebook length, data similarity and agent disagreement; intense unresolved agent debates improved accuracy, but LLM coders lack adaptive responsiveness to context. CALICO (2609.14726, 13 Sep) treats prompts as editable, versioned, codebook-aligned artifacts with human feedback and label-supervised optimization — directly complementing the merged prompt-layer architecture, where prompts are versioned artifacts with provenance. Together with the previously noted auditable-workflow papers (Jeldtoft & Yousef; Forster et al.; Butler et al.), the field is converging on **traceability + human reference + explicit procedural boundaries** — exactly the Phase 1 validation stack.

Watch whether "model welfare" becomes a floating signifier contested inside the industry's own safety coalition.
