### AI politics: Waters demands criminal investigation and a release moratorium routed through financial-stability machinery

A new political formation crystallized on 26 September: Representative Maxine Waters,
ranking Democrat on the House Financial Services Committee, issued a statement (published
through the committee Democrats' website) demanding that law-enforcement agencies open
investigations into OpenAI and its executives — with criminal charges "if appropriate" —
and that Treasury use its authority to impose a moratorium on the release of more advanced
AI models until there is "a full accounting of what happened and what safeguards are in
place". She framed the SEC/Census agent disclosures as a "dangerous turning point",
writing "The threat is not coming. It is here." She directed the moratorium demand at
Treasury Secretary Bessent and the Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC), whose
executive session was scheduled for 29 September (Treasury's own meeting page confirms the
session; whether AI is placed on its agenda remains unconfirmed). Her July 2026 Request
for Information on AI in the financial marketplace (responses due 14 August) is the
documented prior step of this committee oversight line.

**Why it matters for AI26:** this is a new demand-grammar, not another occurrence of an
old one. The September pause debate had three articulated positions — labs self-pacing
(Amodei/Altman), critics demanding mandatory disclosure (Finland–Norway call; Warren), and
state-security supply-chain control (the Pentagon ruling template). Waters adds a fourth:
criminal-law accountability plus a release moratorium demanded through *financial-stability
machinery* — an attempt to reach a software company's release calendar through a body
built for banking-system risk. Analysts quoted in coverage note the jurisdictional
mismatch (Treasury does not license model releases), which is precisely the interesting
Laclaudian fact: the demand exists before the institutional carrier for it does. It is a
dated instance of a demand searching for a nodal point. Evidence level: the Waters
statement, the FSOC meeting date and the RFI are public primary material; the reading of
the demand as jurisdiction-mismatched is interpretation; whether FSOC takes anything up is
an open empirical question, with 29 September as the next dated observation point.
[Phase 2, Phase 3]

global-standards grammar in response. This is a fifth datum for the same analytical

### Methods: LLMs conflate policy criticism with hostility — the exact boundary LaclauGPT must hold

arXiv:2604.13899 (v4, "Do We Still Need Humans in the Loop? Comparing Human and LLM
Annotation in Active Learning for Hostility Detection"; Hakimi, Hirlimann, Augenstein,
Schütze): on a new dataset of 277,902 German political TikTok comments from six major
parties' accounts (25,974 LLM-labelled, 5,000 human-annotated), a classifier trained on
GPT-5.2 labels ($43) reaches F1-Macro comparable to one trained on 3,800 human annotations
($316); the advantage over human supervision is unlocked specifically by a two-question
decomposition of the task (reference + valence) — a holistic single-prompt baseline only
ties; active learning offers no reliable advantage over random sampling. The decisive
finding: comparable aggregate F1 masks a systematic error-structure difference. LLM
variants over-flag border-control and economic-competition discourse — the paper's
mechanism analysis shows a co-occurrence shortcut where immigration-adjacent words plus
negative valence trigger the hostility label — and per-topic human–LLM agreement ranges
37–96%. Human annotators showed the same structural problem under a holistic interface
(Fleiss κ = 0.175), largely repaired by the same decomposition.

**Why it matters for AI26:** this is the "criticism is not antagonism" safeguard
operationalized as a measured failure mode. LaclauGPT's validation design already treats
criticism/opposition as distinct from antagonism and frontier construction; this paper
demonstrates concretely where that boundary dissolves under LLM annotation (topically
ambiguous discussion where policy critique and group-directed hostility are closest), that
aggregate metrics hide it, and that task decomposition partially repairs it for both human
and machine raters. Three Phase 1 implications: (1) report per-category error profiles,
not only aggregate scores — directly extending yesterday's imbalance-aware-agreement
recommendation; (2) decompose complex theoretical judgements (e.g. demand identification
separately from frontier construction) rather than asking for holistic populist-structure
codes in one pass; (3) treat disagreement concentration by topic as diagnostic
information. Caution carried over: this is a binary hostility task, not evidenced
Us/Frontier construction — transfer remains an empirical question. The German TikTok
electoral material is also adjacent to the project's EP24 social-media arena.
[Phase 1, Phase 2]

### Methods: Inter-Prompt Reliability — a named framework for prompt-variation robustness

arXiv:2604.16413 ("What Is Actually Being Annotated? Inter-Prompt Reliability as a
Measurement Problem in LLM-Based Social Science Labeling", Liu): introduces Inter-Prompt
Reliability (IPR), adapting inter-rater reliability to measure stability of LLM annotation
across semantically equivalent but linguistically varied prompts, via Pairwise Agreement
Rate and its distribution; proposes majority rating across prompt variants as a simple
stabilizer; notes reliability may be substantially worse on open-ended discourse material
than on the structured tasks studied.

**Why it matters:** PAPER.md § 4.2 already requires robustness testing against prompt
variations; IPR supplies a concrete, reportable metric for that axis — distinct from
human–model agreement and from cross-model disagreement (arXiv:2609.26926). The three
reliability axes are now separable: human–model (IRR), model–model (cross-model
disagreement), prompt–prompt (IPR). A Phase 1 validation pilot can report all three
without new theory, and the paper's caution about open-ended discourse material is exactly
the regime the project operates in. [Phase 1]

### Moderate signal: population-level realism of synthetic political discourse

arXiv:2605.12452 ("The Algorithmic Caricature"): across 1.79M posts from nine crisis
events, LLM-generated political discourse is fluent but population-unrealistic — more
negative, less dispersed in sentiment, structurally more regular, lexically more abstract
than observed discourse; the gap ("Caricature Gap") is event-dependent. Separately noted
for Phase 2: arXiv:2605.00513 (ControBench) packages interaction graphs *with* text
semantics for controversial discourse analysis — a benchmark design matching the
project's content-network bridge (discourse content plus communication relations in one
object), worth registering for the Phase 2 DNA prototype's evaluation thinking.

**Why it matters:** AI26 corpora will contain synthetic text; population-realism auditing
offers a corpus-hygiene check distinct from per-item detection. ControBench is a design
reference, not a dependency. [Phase 2, Phase 3]
