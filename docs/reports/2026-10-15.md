## AI26 external signals (2026-10-15)

One line each; only material that is new against the 2026-10-14 sections.

Science and methods:

- *Agreement Without Alignment: Label-Free Rubric Clarification Increases
  LLM-Judge Consensus but Not Agreement with Humans* (arXiv **2610.13303**, 09
  Oct) - preregistered, 129,770 judge requests: repairing the rubric clauses
  judges disagreed on (no human labels used) raised inter-judge Krippendorff's
  alpha by 0.069 but moved agreement with expert ratings by nothing detectable
  (Kendall tau -0.007, 95% CI -0.037 to 0.021). The sharpest available warning
  that the agreement statistics the paper's section 4.2 proposes can be raised
  without becoming more valid. **credible (preprint, preregistered)**.
- *Nobody Truly Agrees on Sentiment* (arXiv **2610.10318**, 07 Oct, TPDL 2026) -
  six human raters, 100 tweets: humans reach only "fair" agreement with each
  other, and fine-tuned Twitter-roBERTa aligns with them better than the three
  LLMs tested. A hard number for the human ceiling in section 4.2's benchmark
  design. **credible (accepted)**.
- *JEV versus LLMs: Accuracy, Cost and Calibration on Seven Political Science
  Replications* (arXiv **2610.06625**, 05 Oct) - explicit choice probabilities
  are better calibrated than an LLM's token probabilities, but there is no cost
  advantage; direct support for the paper's "model confidence is not a calibrated
  probability of correctness". **credible (preprint)**.
- *Boundaries Agree, Labels Do Not* (arXiv **2610.04370**, 03 Oct) - one expert
  reader reproduced segment boundaries months apart while naming the segments
  differently, in every text; the LLMs showed no such boundary/label asymmetry.
  A usable contamination and data-quality check for the human-coded benchmark.
  **credible (preprint)**.
