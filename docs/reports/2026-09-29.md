### Methods: QuanReview — a reference implementation of disagreement-targeted adjudication

arXiv:2609.35685 (28 September; Musacchio, Giner Pulero, Castañeda, Couriel, Mejova,
Beiró): QuanReview, an open-source system for offline, auditable reconciliation of human
and LLM span annotations. It aligns two annotation streams at character level, resolves
unambiguous cases by an explicit and logged policy, routes candidate conflicts to a
browser-based adjudication interface (accept either side, build field-level hybrids, flag
for re-annotation), manages campaigns with configurable multi-annotator redundancy,
computes agreement at document and span level, auto-merges unanimous documents, and
exports the corrected layer in the original file format. On a 4,457-record humanitarian
benchmark plus an LLM extraction stream: 8% of documents auto-merged, policy decisions on
1,513 records, human attention concentrated on 3,131 candidate conflicts (mean 5.4 per
reviewed document).

**Why it matters:** this is close to a reference implementation of the
disagreement-targeted adjudication element recorded in the 26–27 September briefs
(disagreement as signal; imbalance-aware metrics; per-category error profiles). The
architecture is transferable even though the pilot is not a span-extraction campaign:
(a) an explicit, logged policy for unambiguous cases mirrors the protocol's claim-status
and attribution preservation; (b) conflict concentration gives "disagreement as signal" a
workable operational meaning — small, bounded, human-attention-indexed sets; (c)
export-in-original-format keeps reconciliation auditable without changing the tree
contract. Worth checking whether an equivalent reconciliation pass already exists in the
project's LLM-vs-human comparison design or whether the pilot would need to build this
layer. [Phase 1]

### Methods: SCALE — a formal basis for selective human escalation

arXiv:2609.28859 (24 September; Ham, Jasin, Yang): "Human-AI-Powered Hypothesis Testing."
Formalizes the pilot's exact situation: LLM as inexpensive judge with noisy, biased
outputs; selective human verification; prescribed type-I/type-II error control. Derives
a cost lower bound and a sequential policy (query AI, send to human, escalate after the
AI report, stop when evidence suffices) that is valid at finite sample sizes and
near-optimal as target errors vanish; extends to an unknown AI-output model via paired
AI-human pilot data.

**Why it matters:** gives the human-review design a citable formal basis for the exact
decision problem the validation pilot faces — which items escalate to human review, when
to stop, at what error guarantee and budget. This moves "human review is not bypassed"
from methodological principle toward a formally analyzed policy class, complementary to
the disagreement-targeted adjudication recommendation. [Phase 1]

### Methods: a synthetic-population validation cluster has emerged

The 26 September brief noted "The Algorithmic Caricature" (arXiv:2605.12452) as a moderate
signal. In the last week a coherent cluster has formed around population realism of
synthetic respondents:

- **Cultural Divergence Preservation** (arXiv:2609.29928, 24 September; Chae, Choi, Lee,
  Im): a reference-light diagnostic (one-time human calibration) separating *cultural
  flattening* (reduced cross-country divergence in LLM-simulated survey populations) from
  *caricature* (increased divergence). Conventional fidelity metrics (Jensen-Shannon
  divergence) stay near-constant through both failure modes; CDP moves monotonically with
  the true divergence manipulation.
- **"Narrowing the Horizon"** (arXiv:2609.35302, 28 September; Peter, Simperl, Devlin):
  measures topic-saliency shifts across model families and shows post-training
  homogenizes topic saliency (generative monoculture), with specialized models partially
  preserving perspective diversity.
- **"Consequential Behaviour and Representational Fairness"** (arXiv:2609.27690, 23
  September; Kutzner et al.): ad hoc comparisons with human surveys test the wrong
  target for applied synthetic research; proposes a validity framework where every claim
  states its correspondence level (predict behaviour? which of four diagnostics —
  location, dispersion, response process, structure — does it cover?), with mandatory
  subgroup-level validity claims and three justice dimensions operationalized as
  measurable quantities; includes a reporting checklist.

**Why it matters:** AI26's synthetic fixtures and corpora need a population-realism audit
axis that the current protocol does not yet name. The cluster supplies a diagnostic
vocabulary (flattening, caricature, saliency homogenization) and a validity-claim
framework (consequential behaviour, subgroup reporting) to borrow for the corpus-synthesis
audit design. Design reference, not a dependency. [Phase 2, Phase 3]
