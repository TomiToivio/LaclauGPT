## AI26 external signals (2026-10-24)

Checked against every section of the 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23 files. Items below
are new to the register.

**News — AI politics and labour (established, primary documents):**

- **Wyden released two bills on 23 Oct that chain labour demands to an
  infrastructure tax.** The *Stability and Opportunity for Workers Act* creates
  income support for up to two years, retraining tuition assistance, and extends
  federal-state Extended Benefits by up to 52 weeks when unemployment reaches
  8.5%. The second bill imposes an excise tax on data-centre operators and bars
  data centres from the research credit, 100% bonus depreciation and Opportunity
  Zone breaks. Why it matters: this is a single legislative act articulating the
  grassroots arena's *two* demand clusters — worker security and data-centre
  cost-bearing — into one parliamentary claim, which is exactly the
  demand-chaining §4.1 says must be demonstrated rather than inferred from shared
  vocabulary. It also gives the corpus a first-party text where "who pays for the
  infrastructure" is the explicit nodal point.
- **The wider bill field now has four competing AI-labour articulations**, per
  CBS (23 Oct): Kelly's *Make AI Work for Americans Act* (5% digital-advertising
  excise, usage tax, 50% excess-profits tax paying into a retraining fund),
  Casar/Foushee/Jacobs' *AI Tax and Work Protection Act* (token-usage tax whose
  rate rises with unemployment), and the bipartisan Warner–Hawley *AI-Related Job
  Impacts Clarity Act* (mandatory AI-layoff reporting to the Department of
  Labor, introduced 2025, House companion July 2026). AP-NORC: ~80% say protecting
  workers in the age of AI is extremely or very important. Why it matters: the
  tax-vs-transparency split, and a Republican co-sponsor on the labour-disclosure
  side, is the kind of cross-cleavage articulation the parliamentary arena exists
  to record. Confidence: *established* (congress.gov and Senate Finance bill text).
- **State-level transparency legislation is moving too**: the New Jersey Senate
  passed the Greenstein–Stack bill on data-centre development transparency, 23 Oct
  (*credible*, single state-level report). Distinct from the registered
  community-benefit story: this is disclosure as legislation rather than as a
  corporate concession.

**Scientific publications (credible preprints):**

- **arXiv 2610.30987, "Interpretive Variation and Discourse Structure in Detecting
  Enthymemes in Controversial Political Discourse"** (Pastor & Oostdijk, Radboud;
  22 Oct 2026). Five annotators per tweet on vaccination and immigration; F1
  plateaus near 0.67 for every fine-tuned model regardless of data size, features
  or label type; the authors propose evaluating predictions against the *full
  distribution* of annotator votes, and show models reliably recognise texts
  *without* an enthymeme while rarely committing on those that contain one. Why it
  matters: an enthymeme is the minimal case of the paper's own object — an
  articulation whose link is not in the text — so this is direct evidence for
  §4.2's requirement that abstention be assessed separately from accuracy, and for
  §4.3's warning that fluent coverage can hide missed articulations.
- **arXiv 2610.15712, "TL;QR: Querying Temporal Discursive Patterns in Textual
  Corpora"** (Hublet et al., ETH Zurich; 11 Oct 2026). A declarative query
  language where an LLM judges only local passage-level predicates and a symbolic
  temporal-logic engine aggregates them, so temporal and relational reasoning
  stays exact and every match links back to the passages and decisions behind it;
  case study on ten years of European Parliament debates (442 593 multilingual
  speeches) finding candidate stance shifts on the GDPR. Why it matters: §4.1
  names temporal sequence as one of the stronger forms of evidence for an
  articulation travelling between arenas, and this is an auditable way to query
  for it in exactly the parliamentary corpus we are now building. Phase 2
  methodological reference, not a Phase 1 claim.

  2606.06715v2 are already in the register and are not restated.
