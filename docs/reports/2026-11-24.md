scheduler: no GPU capacity for gemma4:12b within 900s (status code: 503)

and the same three tasks are re-claimed and re-invalidated in every cycle:

Each attempt waits the provider's 900 s scheduler timeout before failing, so
three tasks consume ~51 min, the fourth hangs, and the cycle dies at 9000 s.

**2026-11-24 06:13:54** - no analysis output for ~9 h 45 min at the time of
measurement, against an otherwise steady 4-9 records/hour all week. Today's

Machine state, read-only: three V100s exist, all near-full (32.5 / 25.7 / 32.2
GB of 32 GB), occupied by three long-running non-AI26 inference services with
~18 h uptime, and the Ollama endpoints themselves have no model resident
(`models: []`). So gemma4:12b cannot be placed, and every analysis call pays a
900 s penalty. This is a GPU allocation conflict, not a code regression, and it
is reported to Data-Analysis with the log lines and the failure documents
(**#569**).

## AI26 external signals (2026-11-24)

Checked against the 22-23 November sections of this directory; nothing already
recorded there is repeated.

- **UN Secretary-General calls for a multilateral AI risk framework within 12
  months - credible (secondary aggregator, 24 November).** Guterres, speaking
  at the Bridgetown Special Summit on AI Safety in Barbados, proposed common
  safety standards, testing procedures and minimum requirements for independent
  supervision, framed around US-China competition. Announced intent, no draft
  text. This is the first *global* tier articulation of the same "standards +
  independent supervision" bundle the labs and US states are separately
  claiming - useful as the third point of the interdiscursive triangle.
- **US export-licence rule reaches data centres in five countries - credible
  (secondary aggregator, 24 November; core rule dated 16 November).** Operators
  in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil now need US licences
  for the most advanced AI chips. It converts the data-centre stratum from a
  siting/energy conflict into a sovereignty/export-control conflict, which is a
  new articulation for the grassroots infrastructure frame.
- **National Governors Association bipartisan principles for frontier AI - credible
  (22 November, surfaced today).** Federal standards for the largest models,
  states keeping consumer, worker, child-safety and anti-discrimination
  authority; independent evaluations and incident reporting federally. A US
  *sub-national* governance voice, and the one US actor type the corpus could
  reach without a browser - but it is not in the source plan, and adding it is
  Tomi's call.
- **SAFE AI Act outline, House bipartisan trio - established (Politico, 19
  November).** Jacobs (D-CA), Maloy (R-UT) and Beyer (D-VA) released a two-page
  outline: published, non-loosenable safety frameworks, CAISI-set minimums,
  pre-deployment evaluation, incident reporting, whistleblower protection, and
  explicitly "a floor - not a ceiling" against federal preemption. Still an
  outline, not bill text. This is the US parliamentary object the arena cannot
  currently observe; it makes yesterday's "Obernolte optimistic before January"
  item concrete.
- **arXiv 2611.09457, "Definitions, Label Names, or Demonstrations? Auditing
  What Governs LLM Annotation" - credible preprint.** Across three
  political-text datasets, models rarely follow *reassigned* definitions:
  predictions track label-name priors and demonstrations, and fine-tuning
  strengthens the original mapping rather than definition adherence. This is
  the sharpest available statement of the failure mode LaclauGPT's validation
  section must rule out - that the codebook label, not the codebook argument,
  drives the output - and it suggests the concrete test: swap two formation
  definitions and re-run, then report the disagreement rate against a paraphrase
  floor.
- **arXiv 2611.08846, "When F1 Is Not Enough: Auditing Codebooks for LLM-Assisted
  Annotation" - credible preprint.** Proposes a pre-deployment codebook audit
  with class-specific metrics, confusion matrices and structured document
  review, and finds some boundary defects are codebook defects that transfer
  across models while others are annotator-specific. Directly transferable to
  the codebook itself: it turns "agreement is low" into "which rule is
  under-specified".
- **arXiv 2611.02273, "When the Text Is Silent: Memory Shortcuts and the Validity
  of LLM Annotation under Evidence Insufficiency" - credible preprint.** With
  conflicting evidence models follow the text, but with *missing* evidence they
  fill in remembered facts (up to +17 p.p. for recognised figures), and 98.6% of
  those labels are factually right yet unsupported. Relevant to AI26's own
  `insufficient_text` abstention class: it is exactly the condition in which
  invented evidence is most likely.
- **Social media: the "common limits" essay is being absorbed inside the labs -
  credible (X threads, unmeasured).** Beyond Altman's endorsement already
  reported, Noam Brown ("everyone deserves to know the trajectory of progress")
  and Boaz Barak (pretraining acceleration "arguably the highest risk in the
  short term") both publicly backed publication, while critics on the same
  threads noted the White House Accords behind it are non-binding. The pacing
  signifier is now being claimed by safety-leaning and capability-leaning
  insiders simultaneously - the cleanest live case of an empty signifier being
  fought over in the elites arena, and it is in the corpus already.
