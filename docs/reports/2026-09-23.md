## Research watch

### High signal: public AI-risk discourse is becoming politically mainstream

A Reuters/Ipsos poll published 22 September reports that 73% of U.S. respondents worry AI companies are not doing enough to prevent potentially catastrophic consequences, 55% support slowing AI development, and respondents strongly prefer safe/responsible development over global competitiveness. This matters for Phase 1 because existential-risk and safety signifiers are no longer confined to specialist communities; they are entering mass political opinion and may articulate with distrust of corporate self-regulation and demands for federal oversight.

At the same time, Donald Trump publicly rejected new AI regulation while saying the Justice Department could intervene if needed and framing AI as a transformation larger than the Industrial Revolution. UN Secretary-General António Guterres called for multilateral AI risk governance and independent oversight. These positions make `safety`, `regulation`, `competition`, `progress`, `catastrophe`, `authority` and `superintelligence` especially valuable floating/contested signifiers to follow empirically rather than treating actors as fixed ideological types.

### High signal for validation: auditable local/human-centred workflows

Jeldtoft and Yousef's 2026 preprint on auditable LLM-supported thematic analysis is unusually compatible with LaclauGPT's methodological direction: preserve interpretive context, maintain traceable source-output links, represent analytical constructs explicitly, constrain LLM inference to interpretive tasks, and support privacy-preserving local deployment. Their evaluation combines structural comparison with human analysis and independent expert assessment rather than relying on agreement alone.

Zupic's 2026 human-centred content-analysis workflow similarly treats annotation, summarisation and extraction as researcher-designed, supervised and validated stages. For Phase 1, these papers support modular provenance and human adjudication; they do not justify replacing the locked Laclaudian method with thematic/content analysis.

For Phase 2 specifically, Randerson et al.'s 2026 DNA study is directly relevant: GPT-4 recovered more than two-thirds of researcher concepts and screened irrelevant material well, but generated enough irrelevant concepts that human review remained necessary. This is a strong empirical warning against allowing an LLM to autonomously construct the DNA codebook or coalition structure.
