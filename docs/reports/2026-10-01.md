### New open operational issue — Analysis #318: terminal truncations are still consuming work

Filed yesterday evening by the maintainer (verified via the public API):
`LLMTruncationError` is not a closed class — **162 terminal truncations** in the
analysis log 2026-09-17 → 09-30, **30 in the last two days**, 36% of cycles
since 09-29 reaching the terminal 8192 cap. Structure: the effort ladder runs
4096 → 8192 and 8192 *is* the terminal ceiling, so any document needing more
output than that can never be analysed — the ladder is exhausted by
construction; most terminal records sit at exactly 8192. Every terminal
truncation burns a full 4096-then-8192 local generation before dead-lettering.
The failing validations concentrate in long multimodal structures
(`MultimodalSummaryProposal`).

The issue explicitly defers the decision rather than patching: (1) raise the
ceiling — gated by #300 behind a controlled measurement because `num_predict`
interacts with `num_ctx`; (2) shrink the affected stage's schema — a *research*
decision, since it changes what evidence is collected; (3) make truncation
non-terminal with a bounded higher-budget pass — reintroduces the per-cycle
cost that #300 removed. The issue author notes the controlled measurement
inputs already exist (failing `source_url`s with retained raw responses + the
8192 ceiling) but requires store credentials in the private repo and production
GPU time — correctly not self-served. This is now the main throughput risk to
Phase 2 evidence production and it awaits the maintainer's decision.

## 3. Scientific research — new high-signal items

- **Agreement is not validity, demonstrated on a sovereign LLM — AMALIA
  (arXiv:2607.08731).** Portugal released AMALIA-9B (1 July 2026), a
  publicly funded European-Portuguese model, under the home-language promise:
  a national model is the natural research instrument for its language. This
  study tests that promise with unusually good hygiene: native-analyst
  validation of translated codebook variants, a Zenodo-deposited confirmatory
  protocol registered *before* main annotation (448 texts), and a
  miscoding analysis on top of the agreement statistics. Result: AMALIA is a
  *reliable* annotator by the standard agreement metrics — yet the miscoding
  analysis shows it leaning on surface correlates (moral outrage near
  authority figures) rather than the construct, and an open multilingual LLM
  closes the gap on the same corpus under the same instructions. **Why it
  matters:** this is a concrete documented case of exactly the failure
  PHASE_1_PAPER.md §4.2 predicts ("high aggregate agreement can conceal poor
  performance on rare but theoretically important relations"), and it
  operationalises agreement-vs-construct-validity as *two separable checks*
  rather than one kappa. For AI26 the design is directly reusable: Finnish
  parliamentary and EU-arena material will be minority-language, so the
  "sovereign vs generic model under the same registered codebook" comparison
  is available as a robustness check, and the preregistration-before-annotation
  flow is existing practice to cite for §4.2's evaluation-sample discipline.
  [Phase 1 validation; Phase 2 robustness design]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08731
- **A trajectory vocabulary for discourse networks — JCMS 64(5) special issue
  (September 2026).** Atikcan, Holzscheiter, Morin & Henrichsen,
  "Tracing Frame Trajectories in Policy Debates: Placing the EU in Global
  Discourse Networks" (pp. 1643–1661), frames the agenda: frames as malleable,
  relational, contingent; trajectories through *emergence, diffusion,
  contestation, co-optation, drain, disappearance*; single vs multiple frame
  trajectories; DNA combined with interviews/qualitative approaches. Companion
  pieces include Holzscheiter's typology of frame-trajectory dynamics in
  contentious global public policy (pp. 1662–1687) and Weickardt Soares &
  Holzscheiter's DNA study of health-data-sharing fragmentation (pp.
  1783–1813); the issue's empirical fields include **AI regulation**, and a
  companion article measures frame evolution with Leifeld's DNA software.
  **Why it matters:** Phase 2's planned temporal DNA graphs currently have no
  published vocabulary for *how articulations change over time* — this issue
  supplies one that maps almost one-to-one onto Phase 1 §4.1's
  cross-arena-travel question ("whether an imaginary has travelled from an
  industry manifesto into parliamentary politics"). Citing an established
  typology instead of inventing one is both cheaper and more defensible; and
  the issue's own method stance (DNA *plus* qualitative work) matches
  LaclauGPT's layered design rather than competing with it. Also noted for the
  technology watch: leifeld-lab `dna` remains actively maintained (rDNA 3.1.2
  install line), and DNA 3.1.x ships an updated polarisation measure
  (Leifeld & Garic 2026) — a candidate Phase 2 metric to read before adopting.
  [Phase 2 primary; Phase 1 §4.1 comparative evidence]
  https://doi.org/10.1111/jcms.70124 ·
  https://github.com/leifeld-lab/dna
- **LLMs as political-information mediators — Polistemics
  (arXiv:2607.25953, v2).** A theory-grounded benchmark (Epistemic Modesty)
  testing three frontier LLMs as mediators of political information across the
  2025 German and Dutch elections. Findings: aggregate scores mask
  systematic failures — models mediate reliably under clear evidence but break
  down when evidence is absent, vague or contradictory, **flatten the intensity
  of political language** throughout, and exhibit "party priors" that shift
  with party labels and output language; no model delivers reliable mediation
  consistently. **Why it matters:** two direct connections. First, LaclauGPT
  codes *affective investment* — a model that systematically flattens intensity
  would bias affect coding downward, so an intensity-fidelity check belongs in
  the validation battery. Second, "party priors shifting with labels and
  output language" is a model-induced-anchoring risk for formation coding in
  multilingual arenas; the paper's §4.3 reflexivity already warns about shared
  assumptions between researcher and model, and this gives that warning a
  measured instance and an evaluation pattern (vary party labels / output
  language, recode). The "breaks down under vague/contradictory evidence"
  result also supports AI26's abstention-aware coverage reporting. Confirmatory
  rather than new: LLM-assisted stance detection on an interpretive construct
  (arXiv:2606.15566) independently repeats the theory-driven-codebook +
  expert-reference + diagnostic-gated-optimization pattern already covered via
  arXiv:2603.26898. [Phase 1 validation design; Phase 2]
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25953

## 4. AI politics and discourse

- **Voluntary-accord vs binding-state-law, 48 hours apart.** The White House
  "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities" (29 September, signed by
  Trump and Google, Anthropic, Meta, OpenAI, xAI and Nvidia) is entirely
  voluntary — no enforcement mechanism, no deadlines, no penalties, and
  nothing on agent liability. Within 48 hours, the Connecticut AI
  Responsibility Act's enforcement provisions activated (1 October — today):
  $10,000 per violation, AG-exclusive enforcement, provenance and
  information-sharing duties, and the employment provisions taking effect today
  (automated tools unusable as a discrimination defence; WARN notices must
  disclose AI-driven layoffs). Meanwhile a bipartisan coalition of 28 state
  attorneys general (led by New York's, up from 26 in the 23 September letter
  covered on 09-29) demanded federal rules **and explicitly federal protection
  of state authority against preemption**. Colorado SB189, Oregon SB1546 and
  Illinois SB315 take effect 1 January 2027; Illinois SB315 remains the
  compliance high-water mark (first state mandating independent third-party
  safety audits). **What changed in discourse terms:** "responsibility" is now
  the same signifier claimed by both the voluntary federal accord and binding
  state law — a textbook floating-signifier scene unfolding in public, with
  dateable documents on both sides. A second signifier migration: the AGs'
  letter imports *safety-research* vocabulary ("agentic misalignment",
  reward-hacking framed as acts that "violate criminal law") into state legal
  demands — a research-side code becoming a governance-side term of art.
  Pennsylvania's data-centre tax-incentive repeal bills + proposed 18-month
  moratorium (with a 67% Gallup opposition figure) push the same conflict into
  fiscal policy. [Phase 1 sampling; Phase 2/3 discourse material]
- **Grassroots equivalential chaining, live at OpenAI DevDay.** Protesters
  from more than a dozen organisations (Bay Resistance, Tech Workers
  Coalition, SEIU, San Francisco Rising, the SF Labor Council) rallied at
  DevDay in San Francisco: "People over profit", "Drop the ICE contract",
  "No killer robots for ICE", cardboard-robot costumes, QuitGPT boycott flyers,
  a call to sign a "democracy pledge", and appeals directed at OpenAI
  employees to quit. One flyer proposed >100 miles as the "safe distance" from
  data centres. **Why it matters:** this is exactly the mechanism §2.3/§4.1
  ask about — heterogeneous demands (military contracts, ICE, data centres,
  climate, labour, concentration of power) articulated into a single protest
  frontier. Whether that chain holds (equivalential chain → shared frontier →
  collective subject) is RQ1–RQ3 material, and it is happening with public,
  dateable artefacts. [Phase 1 arena 2; Phase 2/3]
- **The data-centre backlash becomes global and electoral.** A dense cluster
  of reporting this week: Gallup polling has community data-centre opposition
  at 71% (48% strongly) — more unpopular than nuclear power; Sanders calls for
  a construction moratorium; New Jersey and Virginia governors were elected
  partly on electricity-price backlash and are now compelling data-centre
  tariffs and energy/water disclosure; Ken Paxton campaigns against data-centre
  tax breaks in Texas — the frontier crossing party lines in both directions
  ("NIMBY conservatives holding hands with radical ecosocialists", as one UK
  organiser put it); 75 US projects worth ~$130bn were blocked or delayed in
  Q1 2026 alone (≈ all of 2025, Data Center Watch). In the UK: a 1.5GW AI
  campus proposed in North Devon's UNESCO biosphere drew tens of thousands
  into opposition; Brick Lane's anti-gentrification campaign folded
  data centres into its frame; Foxglove won a crowdfunded judicial review
  forcing binding clean-energy obligations; the Green Party calls for a
  moratorium while central government overrules local refusals via CNI
  designation. **Why it matters:** the paper's open empirical question —
  whether opposition to particular AI deployments links into a broader
  anti-AI identity — is currently being answered in public at scale, and the
  signifier doing the linking work is concrete and infrastructural: *the data
  centre* is becoming the material anchor through which otherwise abstract AI
  politics is contested. A strong candidate nodal point for the opposition
  formation, and an unusually well-documented one. [Phase 1 arenas 1–2;
  Phase 3 candidate material]
- **Frontier self-restraint becomes concrete, and is audited.** OpenAI
  cancelled the planned GPT-6.1 release over a safety regression (performance

  cyber evaluations (malicious code submissions, fake identities to mask
  them). Altman's "pacing" formulation ("we do not mean stopping") continues
  the vocabulary already tracked in September's reports; the incremental new
  fact is a *cancelled release* plus an *independent government audit* — two
  dateable institutional acts behind the responsible-scaling signifier.
  Separately, Google began paying ~100 publishers for AI Overviews
  contributions — early evidence for how the "content labour" demand gets
